## Reading the result

- **The primary metric misses its target badly on the newest blind set.** The headline is the v3 matcher, scored once on holdout_v3, which its author never opened. Recall is 0.605 (26 of 43) against a 0.95 target. Precision is 0.963, which passes. The misses are listed with the labeler's rationale on the app's Match Quality page.
- **Why recall fell: the set changed, not the matcher.** v1 and v2 were scored on sets made mostly of single-ingredient items. holdout_v3 is 53% combinations, and recall there splits by product type: single ingredient 14/16, combinations 12/23, premixes in D5W or saline 0/4. The known causes are multi-strength combinations, diluent words in premixes, a brand-mapping bug on combinations, and brands absent from the snapshot (ML HANDOFF, v3 error analysis). The split is the Principal Engineer's post-hoc classification by item text. It is not a frozen label.
- **The matcher fails safe.** Precision stays between 0.957 and 0.983 on all five scored sets, seen and unseen. When it is unsure, it misses an item rather than putting a wrong drug in the queue. That is the right failure for a pharmacist's queue, but the misses are real. Any fix needs a new blind set, because holdout_v3 is now seen.
- **History, not headline.** v1 on the gold set: recall 0.877. v2 on holdout_v2: 0.936. Both were blind when they ran. The v3 scores on gold and holdout_v2 (0.983 and 0.936) are labeled "seen, optimistic" and are never compared with the target.
- **The comparison is a fair baseline.** A base-name search (strip salts and forms, then compare names) gets precision 0.579 and recall 0.234 on the v2 holdout (v3 holdout: 0.571 / 0.093). The naive exact-name search gets 0 because openFDA `generic_name` includes the dosage form. Both are shown. The claim is the absolute target, not the lift.
- **Day one is a baseline, not a queue (ADR 007).** There is one live snapshot, so nothing can be "since yesterday". The daily queue is empty by design, and guardrail (b) is N/A. The pharmacist gets a one-time review list: 73 formulary items (item grain), ranked and shown 40 at a time. The 401 matched records that are not actionable (for example Current and Available) are on the Watch list. Under the old rule the first day would have been a 663-item queue. From the second daily pull, (b) is measured on the uncapped actionable queue, with the Watch-list size reported beside it.
- **The controls hold.** The chain verifies. No action ran under a role that is not allowed. Role violations were refused and logged.
- **Time to first action is simulated.** It shows the measurement works end to end. It says nothing about real pharmacists.
